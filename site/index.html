How to use it

Running in two minutes

Start it on your PC

Install Node.js, then npm install and npm start. No account, nothing in the cloud.

Turn on telemetry

In F1 26: Settings → Telemetry → UDP On, port 20777, format 2025 or later.

Open it beside you

Scan the QR code it prints with a tablet or phone on the same Wi-Fi. Then drive; it talks when it matters.

How it works

From a packet to a call, in under a millisecond

Six steps, run ten times a second. Follow one call through them: the car behind closing in.

  1. The game

    F1 26 sends telemetry over your network, up to 60 times a second.

    Lap Data packet
    1,285 bytes · 22 cars

  2. Decode

    17 binary packet types, read byte by byte. Corrupt ones are dropped.

    car 7 · P8
    gap ahead 612 ms

  3. Track the field

    Every car's laps, gaps and tyres, on game time. A flashback rewinds it.

    SAINZ 0.6 s behind
    closing 0.3 s / lap

  4. Analyse

    Measures pit loss, fits tyre wear, simulates every stop, scores the chance of a pass.

    pass chance 41%
    likely into turn 1

  5. Decide

    Rules turn it into one call and a radio line. The most urgent one wins.

    DEFEND
    watch turn 1

  6. Your screen

    Sent to any browser on your Wi-Fi, 10 times a second, and read out loud.

    "Sainz 0.6 behind,
    defend turn 1."

Everything runs on your PC. The example numbers are illustrative; the steps are the real ones (one call traced through the code).

The job

What a race engineer has to answer

A driver can't read a spreadsheet at 300 km/h. The engineer's job is to turn the whole field's data into a few answers, at the moment they matter. These are the questions the tool answers, and what each needs from the game.

The data layer

How the data arrives, and what happens to it

F1 26 broadcasts binary UDP packets to port 20777: 16 kinds, each with the same 29-byte header. Nothing is requested; the game sends and the bridge listens. These are the rates and sizes from a real 44-minute race at Barcelona.

Decode defensively

Each parser takes the per-car struct size from the packet's length, not a constant: F1 26 inserted fields mid-struct, and a layout change costs one warning, not a crash. A non-finite float marks the packet corrupt. Random, truncated and bit-flipped packets are fuzzed through the whole pipeline in the tests.

Model the field on game time

Every car is kept: its last packets, 12 laps of history with pit and safety-car laps flagged, gaps sampled once a second of game time - so a closing rate means the same at 20 Hz or 60 Hz, and a pause doesn't skew it.

Record everything, replay exactly

A capture saves every packet with its arrival time (.f1log.gz, ~300 MB a race). Replays run the same pipeline on the recording's clock, so a bug seen on Saturday can be reproduced, fixed and tested on Sunday, call for call.

Datasets from the raw stream

Captures stay local; small datasets built from them are committed: one row per race, per car per lap (1,631 laps), and every call made. CI scores the models on them.

Architecture

Five layers, wired once

Each layer only reads from the one above it. The live bridge, the replayer, the capture analyzer, the dataset builder and every test run the same createPipeline(), so none of them can drift from what the driver sees.

1udp/Ingest: the socket and one parser per packet id. Bytes in, plain objects out.
2state/The field: every car, updated packet by packet, rewound by flashbacks. Facts only.
3analysis/ · ml/Numbers: pure functions for pit timing, the race plan, rival stops, damage cost, tyre wear, overtake odds.
4engine/Decisions: runs the analysis every 100 ms and turns it into the call (PUSH / HOLD / BOX) and radio cards that settle before they're said.
5server/Out: one JSON snapshot, pushed over a WebSocket at a fixed 10 Hz. The browser formats; it never decides.
~0.5 msCPU per engine tick, for 22 cars
< 48 KBper snapshot, sent 10 times a second
2runtime dependencies; no build step, no database, no cloud

Tyre wear and rival stops are scored in CI on every push: a change that makes either worse fails the build. Wear rates carry a 90% range from a car-level bootstrap, and the stop plan is raced 1,000 times across that range.

Case study

One race, six fixes

Barcelona, 23 laps, recorded on 17 October. The capture was replayed through the engine, every call graded against the laps that followed, and each problem became a failing test before it became a fix.

  • The call flickered

    "Undercut on" and "Tyres and fuel OK" swapped 7 times in 3.5 minutes as the gap hovered at the threshold.

    Fix

    A calm call has to hold 5 s before it replaces another. BOX, PIT and a safety car still come at once.

    7 → 2 calls

  • An undercut from P8 "rejoins P21"

    The rule checked for clean air but never whether you'd come out ahead of the car you were undercutting.

    Fix

    Only when fresh tyres gain more before they stop than the gap to them; it says by how much.

    gain > gap

  • ERS advice every 10-20 s

    The 2026 battery swings 20%+ inside a lap, so "Boost" and "battery low, save it" took turns.

    Fix

    Judged on the battery at the line, not the swing within the lap.

    74 → 46 a race

  • "You gain 5.46 s" into turn 1

    The pit lane runs over that stretch, and a pit lap was in the averaged mini-sectors.

    Fix

    Pit and safety-car laps left out; the median, not the mean.

    5.46 s → 0

  • Two defend calls taking turns

    The battle call and the older "right behind you" card spoke separately: 45 of 55 key radio calls.

    Fix

    One defend card; the battle call speaks on it.

    1.4 → 1.2 /min

  • "Disqualified" in qualifying

    The game sent penalty type 6 to a driver who went on to set the fastest lap - its meaning changed in F1 26.

    Fix

    Kept off the radio, like type 16 before it. Only penalties whose meaning is certain are said.

    off the radio
How it's built

Tests that find bugs, not tests that agree with the code

Test first, from the intended behaviour

Over 250 tests. Each new rule's test is written from what it should do and seen to fail before the code exists; assertions are never edited to pass. Known weaknesses stay in the suite as todo tests.

Mutation-checked

A test only counts once it fails on a planted bug. In the fixes above, 9 of 13 planted bugs were caught at first; each survivor got a test, and all 13 are caught now.

Gates in CI

Coverage can't drop below 88% of lines; the model backtests can't get worse; a tick-time budget and a contract test that tracks every snapshot field a widget reads.

Real packets, real races

Parsers are pinned on packets captured from F1 26; scenario tests replay a scripted race and check each call follows its cause - and never comes before it.
