# Overtake model: LightGBM against the baseline

_Generated by `python ml/overtake_lgbm.py` - do not edit by hand._

**Question:** will the car directly behind pass the car ahead within 10 s? Same samples as the shipped model ([ml-overtake-model.md](ml-overtake-model.md)), exported with `npm run train:overtake -- --samples datasets/overtake-samples.csv.gz`.

**Data:** 193,612 one-second samples of close battles from 10 race sessions, 9,930 followed by a pass (base rate 5.1%).

**Method:** leave one race out. Each race is predicted by models trained on the others only; LightGBM picks its number of trees by early stopping on one of the training races. Its settings were fixed before any test race was scored.

**Verdict:** LightGBM beats the gap on its own: AUC 0.860 against 0.838, better on 9 of 10 races, and the 90% range of the gain (+0.014 to +0.030) is above zero. The app runs it (88 trees, trained on every race).

## Held-out scores (all races pooled)

| Model | AUC | Log loss | Brier | Brier skill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline: logistic, log gap only | 0.838 | 0.1602 | 0.0422 | +0.133 |
| Logistic, all 9 features | 0.842 | 0.1586 | 0.0419 | +0.138 |
| LightGBM, all 9 features | 0.860 | 0.1533 | 0.0411 | +0.156 |

AUC: 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 a perfect ranking. Brier skill: the improvement over always predicting the base rate (above zero is better than that).

AUC gain of LightGBM over the baseline, 90% range from 1,000 resamples of whole races: **+0.014 to +0.030**.

## Caveats

- Mostly AI drivers: this is how the game's AI battles.
- Samples a second apart in the same battle are not independent, which is why the range above resamples whole races rather than rows.
- The game's OVTK event fires on any position swap, including a car ahead running wide or pitting.
